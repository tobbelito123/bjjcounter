BJJ Submissions Counter vs BJJBuddy

Both track your Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu progress, but they're aimed at different habits and different budgets. Here's the honest breakdown.

BJJBuddy is a native iOS and Android app built around a full training journal: class notes, competition results, submission and sweep charts, video recommendations based on your stats, Apple HealthKit integration, and stat-sharing with friends. Core use is free to start, with a Pro subscription (around $5.99/month or $39.99/year at time of writing) unlocking the full analytics and history.

BJJ Submissions Counter does one thing: it counts the submissions you land in live rolling, broken down by which belt you hit them against. No session notes, no charts, no subscription, no app-store install. It's a website you open, tap a technique, tap a belt, and you're done — built for logging something between rounds without breaking your flow or your wallet.

Why beginners often reach for BJJ Counter first

A subscription training journal is a real commitment before you know if BJJ is going to stick for you. BJJ Submissions Counter skips all of that: no app-store install, no sign-up, no monthly fee, just open it and start tapping. It answers the one question that matters most in the first months: which submissions are actually working for you, and against who? That's easy to feel mid-roll and surprisingly easy to forget by the time you get home.

Nothing stops you from using both. Plenty of people start with BJJ Counter for the free, frictionless tally, then move to a full journal app like BJJBuddy once they know they're training for the long run and want the deeper analytics.
