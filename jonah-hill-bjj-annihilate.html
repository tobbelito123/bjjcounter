Jonah Hill's "Annihilate" Moment, and What It Actually Means for Beginners

A clip from a podcast recorded months earlier suddenly went viral in August 2026. Here's the real story, plus the actually useful takeaway buried in it.

In April 2026, on the SmartLess podcast, Jonah Hill was asked about people who still picture him as "the fat guy from Superbad." His response, paraphrased from the now-viral clip:

"I would annihilate you, dude."

Jonah Hill, on SmartLess

August 2026 Clip goes viral

It wasn't an idle brag. Hill has actually been training Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu since 2018, when he started at age 35, well past the age a lot of people assume is "too late" to begin. He trains regularly at Clockwork Jiu-Jitsu in New York City, drilling four to five sessions a week under Josh Griffiths, a third-degree black belt who has coached and rolled with top UFC competitors.

The pushback (and why it's a fair point)

The clip didn't go unchallenged. Coaches connected to the MMA world pushed back publicly, arguing that private lessons with a coach are a very different animal from live sparring against a fully resisting opponent, and that confidence built in one doesn't automatically transfer to the other.

That's a fair distinction, and it's exactly the distinction BJJ Submissions Counter is built around. Drilling and private lessons teach you technique. Live rolling is where you find out what actually works against someone trying to stop you. The app only counts submissions you land in that second category: real, resisting rolls, not drilled reps in isolation.

The actually useful takeaway

You're probably not going to have a viral podcast moment about your own BJJ progress. Most of us won't. Progress in this sport is quiet, slow, and easy to lose track of, which is exactly why it's worth keeping score of your own small wins, especially in the first year or two when it's hardest to tell if you're actually getting better. That first live-roll armbar you land against a blue belt is a real, countable data point, even if nobody's filming it for a podcast. It's free to start tracking, and you won't need to sign up for anything to do it.
