BJJ Submissions Counter vs BJJ Notes

One's a training journal, the other's a stopwatch for your submissions. Here's how to tell which one you actually need.

BJJ Notes is a training-journal app: log sessions, set progress goals, get weekly recaps, and on some plans get "Coach Mode" feedback. It generally runs on a free account with optional paid tiers, and it's built for practitioners who want to reflect on their training in writing.

BJJ Submissions Counter skips the journaling entirely and focuses on one number that's oddly hard to track in your head: how many times you've actually finished each submission, and against which belt. No writing, no account, just tap the technique, tap the belt, and you're done.

Different tools, different moments

Journaling is great after class, when you've got time to sit down and reflect. But mid-roll, or right after class while you're packing your bag, you want something that takes two taps, not two paragraphs. That's the gap BJJ Submissions Counter fills. It's especially useful early in your BJJ journey, when you're still figuring out which techniques are actually working for your body versus which ones just feel cool in the drilling line.
